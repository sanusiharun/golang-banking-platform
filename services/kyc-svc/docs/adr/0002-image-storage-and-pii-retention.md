# MinIO for image archival, encrypted NIK, defined retention

A KTP image plus its extracted NIK is sensitive PII (national ID, name, address, face photo). No other service in this platform stores binary blobs or has a purge/retention mechanism — every other service just persists structured rows to Postgres indefinitely. Getting this wrong is expensive to fix retroactively: PII stored in plaintext or kept forever is a compliance problem you can't undo after the fact, and this platform has no object storage dependency to reuse.

We decided to archive the raw KTP image in MinIO (S3-compatible, new container on `banking-net`, ports `9056`/`9057`) rather than as a Postgres BYTEA column, to avoid bloating DB backups with binary data. The extracted NIK is encrypted at rest using `pkg/crypto` (the same RSA/AES utilities already used for JWT subject encryption), reused rather than inventing a second encryption scheme. Retention is tied to the account lifecycle rather than a fixed window from verification time: the raw image is kept for as long as the account it verified remains open, plus a 5-year statutory tail after closure, matching typical Indonesian financial record-keeping expectations. This means `kyc-svc` needs an account-closure signal from `account-svc` to start the tail countdown — retention is not something `kyc-svc` can compute from its own data alone.

Considered and rejected: storing images as encrypted Postgres BYTEA — avoids a new infra dependency, but doesn't scale for binary blobs and mixes storage concerns no other service here has to deal with.
